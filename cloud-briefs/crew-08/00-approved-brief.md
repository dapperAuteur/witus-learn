# Brief: The Show Crew, How the A1, A2, V1, EVS, Graphics and the TD Make a Live Game (CREW-08)

**Status:** approved 2026-10-26 for build, as CREW-08. Public; held for a qualified reviewer with sports truck
credits (an A1, V1, replay operator or technical director). Research tier 1. Employers (NFL Media, Fox) and makers
(EVS, Vizrt, Sony, Riedel, Calrec, and others as sources describe them) are named as cited worked examples, the way
CREW-05 named the NBA. This is the first of a planned group: per-chair courses (audio, video shading, replay,
graphics) follow later, each with its own brief.

## 1. Description

A learner can walk into a sports truck or studio control room and say who sits where, what each crew role on the
headset does during a live game (the A1 and A2 in audio, the V1 and V2 in video, the replay and playout operators,
the graphics operator and broadcast associate, the technical director, the utility and the floor crew), what gear
each one runs, how they talk to each other on comms, and what each one must have ready before air. They can follow
one play from the camera to the replay to the graphic to the cut, and say who acts at each step. They can read a
real posting for any of these jobs, tell what it asks for, and see the path from utility to the chair. It teaches
the craft each chair needs at a working level, not a vendor's operator certification.

## 2. Outline

1. **The room and the chain of command:** the truck and the studio control room; producer, director, associate
   director, technical director; the rundown and the clock. Link `broadcasting-break-in` lessons
   `landscape-and-roles`, `the-switcher-and-control-room` and `game-day-roles` for the overview; do not repeat them.
2. **Audio: the A1 and the A2 (and A3).** What each does as sources state it; mics, RF and IFB; comms panels and
   intercom; the mix and its formats; networked audio. Link CREW-02 and CREW-00 for the craft and cables.
3. **Video: the V1 and the V2.** Shading (iris, gamma, black level, color balance) so cuts match; camera chains,
   CCUs and RCPs; waveform monitors and vectorscopes; robotic cameras. What a V2 does, as sources state it. Link
   CREW-04's shading lesson and CREW-00's camera cable lessons.
4. **Replay and playout:** the replay operator on a replay server (ingest, clips, the replay, rolling on cue);
   playout of packages and elements; the TOC operator who books and checks feeds. Link CREW-05 and CREW-07.
5. **Graphics:** the graphics operator, the broadcast associate who checks names and numbers, real-time systems,
   designers and animators, and the virtual enhancement operator who places the first-and-ten line.
6. **The floor and the support crew:** utility, camera operators, stage or floor manager, lighting director. Link
   CREW-01 and CREW-03.
7. **How it fits together:** comms channels and who hears whom; the director's calls (the standby, ready and take
   sequence) as sources state them; the count back; checks before air; one play followed through every chair; a
   mock segment; who fixes what when something breaks on air.
8. **Getting hired:** the postings' pay and experience (as posted, with dates), freelance and staff, the season,
   the path from utility to a chair, where to train; link `broadcasting-break-in` lesson
   `unions-networking-first-job` for unions.
9. **Capstone:** write a crew sheet and comms plan for one game, with every role's pre-air checklist, cited to
   postings and manuals; or follow one role through a game day.

About 9 sections, 26 to 30 lessons, section quizzes and a final.

## 5. Rules for the build

- Teach only VERIFIED facts from `research/`. A job posting is a primary source for what that employer asked on that
  date; pay is always "as posted on <date>", with a unit only where the posting states one (several NFL postings give a bare dollar figure), never a market rate.
- Common crew usage that no source states (for example "the A1 mixes the show") is not asserted until a source
  states it; the research files mark these.
- Makers' operator screens and certification content stay out; name products only as sources describe them.
- No private person is named. Name no individual crew member, even if a trade article does, unless the fact needs it.
