## 1. Description

A learner can explain how a Black neighborhood's beach in Indianapolis came out of a flood levee, a dam built for
a white park and a sandbar, and was then made official by a city that kept its other beaches and pools closed to
Black residents; read a National Register nomination and a 1941 newspaper complaint (as the nomination reports
it) as evidence of who decided what; compare Belmont with contested pools, beaches and parks in other US cities;
and visit the site from public ground without entering the river.

## 2. Outline (built 2026-11-01; 26 lessons; six sections)

- **Section 1 · Then:** owned, assigned, claimed (links REST-00 and REST-01); 1913 to 1917, flood, levee, the
  Emrichsville Dam built for Riverside Park, and the sandbar; the west side fills (ward five, Haughville); 1927,
  the country club the Parks Board would not allow (two versions taught side by side; co-occurrence drill with
  Fox Lake); key terms; quiz.
- **Section 2 · Built:** 1930 to 1932, Dunbar Park, the name nobody used; August 8, 1936, the formal opening and
  what "formal" added; ball fields, merchants and the Baby Lincolns, what the *Recorder* chose to report; quiz.
- **Section 3 · Who decided:** four public bodies (parks board, board of public works, board of health, and later
  the courts and the legislature); primary-source exercise on the June 1941 complaint (content note: two
  drownings, unnamed, as the nomination records them); one pool across town, Douglass Park (opened 1921; pool
  1922 or 1927); how Indianapolis desegregated its water and what it left alone (Riverside 1919 to 1962 or later;
  the 1958 wading pools; the private Riviera Club and *Bates v. Riviera Club*, with the 1979 drowning unnamed);
  quiz.
- **Section 4 · Now:** 1959 to 2026, the expressway, Rev. Mozel Sanders Park (2008), the Friends (2020), the
  pop-up park (2021), the 2026 listing as "Belmont Beach" and what the listed boundary holds (open); now (360
  tour, forthcoming), public ground only, the river is not for swimming.
- **Section 5 · The same fight in other cities:** 1949, Gary's lakefront and Washington's Anacostia pool; Kansas
  City, Swope Park and *Kansas City v. Williams* (1953), read the opinion; Cincinnati's Coney Island and Marian
  Spencer (1952 to 1961) and Pittsburgh's Highland Park pool; wade-ins and carousels: Rainbow Beach (1960, 1961),
  Glen Echo (1960), Gwynn Oak (1963), St. Augustine's Monson Motor Lodge pool (1964, content note); map; quiz.
- **Section 6 · Write:** assignment (one beach, one board decision, one newspaper page); what this course cut and
  the sources nobody has opened; final quiz.

## 5. Course or lesson?

**Its own course** (BAM, answer 1). Belmont was assigned by a city, not owned, so adding it to `where-we-rested`
would break that course's one claim; the nomination carries four sections; the US cases share Belmont's
mechanism and are each too thin to stand alone. Rubric: A1, A2, A5, A7 pass; A3 pass with correction (city-owned,
not Black-owned); A4 met by quoting no living private person; A6 decided in part (drownings and the Monson acid
in, with a content note and no graphic images; Fairground Park, Belle Isle and Biloxi held); B 3/3/3/3/3/2 = 35;
form C6.

**Corrections found in the build session.** (1) The 2024 form says the beach strip and parkway were already part
of the Indianapolis Park & Boulevard National Register district (Sec. 7 p. 6; one contributing resource
previously listed). (2) "Offered Belmont Beach instead" is the Scripps reporter's narration, not a quotation of
the Friends' director. (3) The Friends' own history page dates the beach to the 1920s.

**Taught as open, with both owners:** when it began (1917 informal, 1936 formal; "early 1930s" in a news report;
"1920s" on the Friends' page); who made it (the form: the city; a news report: business owners "offered" it); the
Fleming episode (threatened condemnation vs. the parks board buying the land); Douglass Park's pool (1922 vs.
1927); what the 2026 listing holds (park and beach nominated; "Belmont Beach" listed); why the city allowed it
(the Friends: pollution made it useless to white residents; the form: "probably" Black demand); Riverside's
picket year (June 1962 on a dated photograph; the end of segregation undated in the source read); Swope's filing
year (1951 vs. 1952); the Monson "manager" vs. "owner". Research checks filed for each that a document can settle.

**Not taught (REPORTED only):** a ban on Black swimming at Belmont and Fall Creek (Haughville riverfront study,
snippet); Riverside "until 1963"; the 1927 *Recorder* pages; the Haughville riverfront study itself.
